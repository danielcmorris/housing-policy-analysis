One repository, every level of government

The Commons brings federal statutes, state legislation, and municipal ordinances into a single structured archive — alongside the economic conditions of each jurisdiction at the time each policy took effect. Bills, enacted laws, regulatory text, administrative data, and the published empirical literature are all held together and linked.

Because the record reaches back roughly fifty years, a proposal introduced today can be set beside comparable measures from other decades and other places — and against what actually happened after they passed.

Contribute

Members can add to the Commons

The Commons grows daily — partly through our own ingestion pipelines, and partly through the researchers who use it. Members may submit their own datasets for inclusion. Each submission is reviewed for provenance, documentation, and methodological soundness before it is cross-referenced into the archive.

balance

No archive of this kind is complete or perfectly comparable — jurisdictions differ, records have gaps, and history rarely runs a clean experiment. The Commons is honest about that. But disciplined comparison against the full weight of prior evidence remains the most rigorous, scientific basis available for anticipating what a policy will actually do.
