Technology

The evidence engine behind every review

We maintain a database of thousands of bills and policy proposals — federal, state, and municipal — held in a cross-referenced vector system, alongside the economic record of each region for the corresponding period. When a new policy comes under review, we can match it accurately to comparable measures already enacted and to how they actually fared.

{{ tp.icon }}

{{ tp.title }}

{{ tp.body }}

This is not a perfect system, and we do not claim it to be. No model can fully predict how a policy will behave. But by grounding every assessment in the largest available record of comparable legislation and its measured outcomes, it offers the most accurate, scientific approach to understanding what a policy is and how it is likely to affect the regions it touches.

Supply-Side Economics CUHPR-2026-0142 Peer Reviewed

The Supply Response to Zoning Reform: Evidence from Minneapolis 2040

Authors  Elena Hargrove, Univ. of Chicago · Paul Nakamura, MIT Published Mar 2026
downloadDownload PDF
smart_toyAsk the AI about this study
databaseSource Data

Abstract

We exploit Minneapolis's 2019 elimination of single-family-only zoning as a natural experiment, comparing permitting outcomes against a synthetic control of 14 comparable metros. Four years post-reform, permitted units rose an estimated 12.4% (95% CI: 7.1–17.6), with effects concentrated in higher-amenity census tracts and muted in low-income peripheries.

Key Findings

  • Zoning liberalization produced a measurable supply response, but with a multi-year lag tied to financing and entitlement timelines.
  • Gains were spatially uneven — triplex and fourplex conversions clustered where land values supported redevelopment.
  • No statistically significant short-run effect on median rents was detected within the four-year window.

Methodology

The authors employ a synthetic control method paired with a staggered difference-in-differences specification to address the timing of complementary reforms. Permitting microdata were sourced from municipal records spanning 2014–2025, deflated to constant 2025 dollars.

Robustness checks include placebo tests on pre-reform trends and alternative donor-pool constructions. The identifying assumption — parallel pre-trends between Minneapolis and its synthetic counterpart — is supported but, as the AI analysis notes below, sensitive to the inclusion of two high-growth Sun Belt metros.

smart_toyAI Methodology Note

The donor pool's sensitivity to two Sun Belt metros warrants a leave-one-out robustness panel. The four-year window may also be too short to capture the full rent response; consider extending the post-period or modeling expected future supply. Continue this analysis →

One repository, every level of government

The Commons brings federal statutes, state legislation, and municipal ordinances into a single structured archive — alongside the economic conditions of each jurisdiction at the time each policy took effect. Bills, enacted laws, regulatory text, administrative data, and the published empirical literature are all held together and linked.

Because the record reaches back roughly fifty years, a proposal introduced today can be set beside comparable measures from other decades and other places — and against what actually happened after they passed.
